Market patterns backtest: NIFTY & Nifty 100, 2015–2026

The only patterns that kept working in both 2015–21 and the unseen 2022–26 data were buying sharp dips in stocks that are still in an uptrend and, to a smaller degree, 52-week-high breakouts on heavy volume while the market is healthy. Most classic index signals were no better than a random day. Falls were best predicted by the market's volatility and situation, not by chart patterns.

2. What came before the falls

Every NIFTY fall of 8% or more since 2015, with the market's condition at the peak. The pattern: big falls started from calm, stretched markets: low volatility, well above the 200-day average, broad participation. Calm is not a sell signal on its own, because most calm periods kept rising, but it is when risk is most underpriced.

When each strategy works

The dip-buy worked in normal markets and recoveries, and failed during long market-wide corrections: FPI selling in 2024–25, the 2022 rate hikes, and the 2020 crash. The breakout lost money in every correction. Neither is a strategy for a falling market.

4. Stock breakouts and breakdowns

Every pattern tested on the 116 stocks. "Excess" is the return above the average stock on the same day, which strips out the market's move and part of the survivor bias. Breakouts only beat the average when volume confirmed them and the market was healthy. Breakouts from tight bases and 52-week-low breakdowns underperformed, so neither is a reliable short. The strongest line is a 5-day fall of more than 12% in a stock above its 200-day average.

What makes an intraday fall more likely (NIFTY)

Chance that NIFTY closes 1% or more below its open. Big gaps in either direction, high volatility, a big move the day before, and a market below its 200-day average all roughly doubled the odds. Small gap-ups (0.2–0.5%) closed below the open more often than above.

Method, definitions and caveats
  • No look-ahead. Signals use the close of the signal day. Trades enter at the next day's open. Intraday filters use the previous day's volatility.
  • Episodes. A signal that fires on several days in a row is counted once per holding period, so cases are not double-counted.
  • Volatility is 20-day realised volatility of NIFTY, annualised (a stand-in for India VIX, which this data lacks). Breadth is the share of the 116 stocks above their 50-day average.
  • Survivor bias. Only stocks in today's Nifty 100 are included. Stocks that crashed and left the index are missing, which overstates breakout and dip-buy results. The excess-return columns compare with the same flawed universe, which removes part but not all of this.
  • Data cleaning. Days with zero prices or one-day jumps over 35% (bad ticks, un-adjusted splits) break a stock's history, so no return is measured across them. LTIM is dropped as a duplicate of LTM.
  • Events were tagged by date range from known history: China devaluation 2015, demonetisation 2016, LTCG budget 2018, IL&FS 2018, COVID 2020–21, FPI selling 2021–22, Russia–Ukraine 2022, rate hikes 2022, Adani–Hindenburg 2023, 2024 election, FPI selloff 2024–25, US tariffs and Operation Sindoor 2025, US–Iran war 2026.
  • Not tested: data after 8 Apr 2026, taxes, and real fills in thin stocks. Run any rule on paper for a few months first.
